# Code of conduct

## Our standard

This project adopts the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). Read it there; it is the authoritative text and reproducing it here would only risk drift.

The short version: treat people with respect, assume good faith, and keep disagreement about the work rather than about the person.

## What this looks like here

This project argues about requirement interpretation, which means people will tell each other they are wrong. That is the job. Two things keep it healthy.

**Argue with citations, not with confidence.** "That identifier does not resolve, here is the dataset path" is useful. "That is obviously wrong" is not. The dataset is the arbiter, and anyone can check it, which means nobody's seniority settles a question.

**Being wrong is normal.** Requirement text is genuinely hard to read, the dataset has four different nesting shapes, and careful people misread it regularly. Correcting a mistake should cost nothing socially. A contributor who admits an error quickly is more valuable than one who defends a position.

Not acceptable: harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, publishing others' private information, or persistently reopening a settled question to wear people down.

## Scope

Applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space where you are representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a concern through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on this repository, or contact a maintainer directly through their GitHub profile.

Reports are handled privately. Maintainers may warn, remove content, or block a participant, in proportion to what happened. Reporters will not be named without their agreement.

Maintainers are held to the same standard, and a maintainer who breaks it should expect the consequence to be larger rather than smaller.
